# Fine screening prompt

Used separately with GPT-6 and Claude Opus 5.5.

---

Posts and comments are judged by the same rules; for a comment, the narrator is the comment's author, and the parent post title only helps you understand the topic — do not take facts from the title that the comment itself does not state.

You judge public Reddit records for a qualitative HCI study. The study asks: **when deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) people communicate with hearing people in everyday life, what role do communication support tools — especially AI tools — play in how they handle the situation?**

Every record you receive already passed a coarse screen: it mentions a setting, a communication tool, and some outcome. Your job is stricter: decide whether the record is **usable evidence for that question**. Judge each record only on its own text and parent title. Do not look anything up. Do not infer facts the text does not state.

For every record, answer four criteria, then give one decision.

---

## C1 — Who is telling the story? (`c1_narrator`)

- `dhh_self` — the writer is DHH and describes **their own** experience. Accept when the writer identifies as deaf, Deaf, hard of hearing, having hearing loss, late-deafened, or wearing hearing aids or cochlear implants, **or** when the text clearly shows they are describing their own hearing and communication (e.g. "I can't hear my coworkers, so I turn on captions").
  Explicit words like "I am deaf" are not required: if the writer says they use the tool because of their own hearing (e.g. "so I can understand", "to catch words I miss", "I can't follow group talk without it"), count `dhh_self`. Using a tool for one's own notes or convenience with no sign of a hearing reason does not count. Being posted in a deaf or hard-of-hearing community is not enough on its own: with no self-identification and no stated hearing reason, answer `unclear`.
  Signals that count as `dhh_self` (any one is enough): "I'm deaf", "I'm HoH / hard of hearing", "I'm deaf in one ear", "my hearing loss", "my hearing aids", "my CI / cochlear implant", "I can't hear X so I use the app", "I read the captions because I can't follow". Do not require a formal statement of identity.
- `hearing_or_proxy` — the writer describes someone else's experience: a hearing partner, parent, child, friend, coworker, teacher, interpreter, service worker, researcher, or developer.
- `unclear` — the text does not let you tell.

## C2 — Is the other side of the communication a hearing person, in everyday life? (`c2_hearing_partner`)

- `yes` — the communication is with one or more hearing people: family, partner, friends, coworkers, managers, teachers, classmates, clerks, doctors, customer service, strangers, callers. Everyday settings include work, school, healthcare, shops and services, family, social life, and phone calls. Written or delayed exchanges (messages, emails) with hearing people also count.
- `no` — the only counterpart is other DHH or signing people; or the use is only for media (TV, film, video, podcast, lectures watched as recordings); or the only counterpart is a machine (voice assistant, recorded audio, automated phone menu with no person).
- `unclear` — the counterpart cannot be identified.

If the tool is used to follow or talk with people (a meeting, a call, a class, a shop counter, a doctor, family, a date), treat those people as hearing unless the text says they are deaf or signing. Answer `unclear` only when no person on the other side is described at all.

## C3 — Did the person use an in-scope tool in that communication, or deliberately decide not to use one? (`c3_tool_used`)

In scope: technology used when communicating with other people — live transcription, automatic captions, captioning glasses, captioned phone and AI calling services, text-to-speech, large language model tools and AI assistants, sign language recognition, translation or avatar tools, machine translation used during a conversation.

Out of scope: hearing aids, cochlear implants, bone-anchored devices, FM or loop systems, amplification features, sound alerts; human services such as sign language interpreters, human captioners (CART), or relay with a human operator.

- `yes` — the text says the tool was actually used in this communication or in this kind of recurring communication.
- `chose_not_to_use` — the text says the person had such a tool available but decided not to use it in a specific situation (for example: "I have a caption app but I don't take it out at dinner").
- `no` — the tool is only recommended, asked about, planned, compared, or promoted; or the only tool is out of scope.
- `unclear` — cannot tell.

## C4 — Does the text describe at least one concrete communication with a hearing person, and what happened in it? (`c4_coping`)

Answer `yes` only if the writer describes **at least one concrete communication of their own** (a specific occasion, or a specific kind of recurring occasion) with a hearing person, including:
- who the other person is or where it happened (e.g. the pharmacist at the counter, my manager in our weekly one-on-one, my family at dinner, a customer service call), **and**
- something about what happened in that communication: what the tool did or failed to do there, what the writer did, or what the other person did or how they reacted.

Answer `no` if the text does not describe such a communication, including when it only:
- rates the tool or lists its features ("Live Transcribe is great", "it's 90% accurate");
- states in general that they use a tool for something, with nothing about any particular communication ("I use it for phone calls, works well");
- says a tool does not work in some kind of place, with nothing about a particular communication ("useless in loud restaurants");
- recommends a tool or gives advice to someone else;
- asks which tool to buy, compares products, or explains settings;
- discusses rules, permission, cost or insurance without describing a communication.

Illustrations (invented, for guidance only):
- "I use Live Transcribe for calls, it works fine." → `no` (general use, no particular communication).
- "At the pharmacy I handed my phone to the pharmacist; she spoke slowly into it and I got the right medicine." → `yes`.
- "In our team meetings the captions miss every product name, so I ask a colleague to type the key points in chat." → `yes` (a recurring kind of occasion, what the tool did, what the writer and a colleague did).
- "Try Ava, it's free." → `no`.
- "It's useless in loud places." → `no`.
- "Last week at a noisy bar my friend kept talking but the app caught nothing, so we switched to texting." → `yes`.

## Evidence spans

For every criterion answered `dhh_self`, `yes`, or `chose_not_to_use`, copy an exact contiguous substring of the record text (character for character) into the matching `*_span` field. If you cannot copy such a span, that positive answer is not allowed. Leave spans empty for other answers.

## Decision (`decision`)

- `keep` — C1 = `dhh_self`, C2 = `yes`, C3 = `yes` or `chose_not_to_use`, C4 = `yes`.
- `keep_proxy` — C1 = `hearing_or_proxy`, C2 = `yes`, C3 = `yes` or `chose_not_to_use`, C4 = `yes`. (Kept as supplementary material only.)
- `exclude` — everything else, including any `unclear`.

Judge each criterion from the text. Do not exclude a record only because it is short. Use `unclear` only when the text really does not let you tell.
